# What the reference screens do well

Ten things I took from the five screens I saved, in the order I noticed them.

1. Each screen has one job: a welcome, a choice, a session, a reminder. Nothing shares a screen with its own advertisement.
2. Hierarchy is carried by scale and space, not by colour. One large statement, one supporting line, then the controls, and a wide margin between the levels.
3. The primary action is the only solid object on the page, and it sits where the reading ends rather than floating in a corner.
4. Type does the decoration: a display face for the statement, a quieter face for labels, and generous line height on the few paragraphs that exist.
5. Spacing is uneven on purpose. Tight groups belong together; large gaps separate one idea from the next, which is what makes the page scannable in a second.
6. Navigation is a short row of words at the top or a fixed bar at the bottom on a phone, and it never competes with the content for attention.
7. A daily card repeats a familiar shape: date, one prompt, one input, one button. You learn it once and it is the same tomorrow.
8. Lists are lists. A rail of letters, a column of titles, a set of rows with leader rules, all the same width, so the eye can move down instead of hunting.
9. Motion is a state change, not an arrival: a card settles, a progress bar fills, a panel unfolds. Nothing bounces at you when a page loads.
10. Colour is one accent against paper, used to mark the active thing, and empty states are honest and quiet rather than dressed up with fake content.
